# Infrastructure Tycoon

A PC-first Unity 2D management simulation about growing a single-PC hosting business into a cloud infrastructure provider.

## Development

Use **Unity 6.6 / 6000.6.9f1** (revision `8c45277fb9c5`), matching `ProjectSettings/ProjectVersion.txt`. This project deliberately uses the installed supported release rather than LTS.

Add this repository root in Unity Hub and open it with that editor. Allow Package Manager and asset import to finish. Commit Assets (including `.meta` files), Packages manifests and lockfile, and ProjectSettings; generated editor folders are ignored.

The foundation uses the built-in renderer, 2D editor defaults, Sprite tooling, uGUI, and Unity Test Framework. Open `Assets/Scenes/Main.unity` and enter Play Mode. The simulation starts on January 1, 1995 at 1x (two real seconds per day). Click PAUSE or 1x/2x/4x/8x to change the rate; the highlighted button and speed label show the selection. Pause preserves fractional-day progress, and selecting a running speed resumes the same session. Cash is live and updates after monthly billing; the status bar reports constrained resources or NO CAPACITY ALERTS. Run the Edit Mode simulation/catalog tests and Play Mode scene suite from Window > General > Test Runner. The same suite can run in a standalone Windows test player to verify real window resizing at 1280x720, 800x600, and 1920x1080; screenshots are saved under Application.temporaryCachePath.

The prototype garage uses reusable Garage, Player, PCStation, and HardwareStation prefabs under Assets/Prefabs. Walk with WASD or arrow keys; diagonal movement is normalized and physics blockers stop the player at walls and the desk. Simulation pause leaves walking active. PCStation/Reach is a separate adjustable trigger from PCStation/Blocker; within reach, the [E] prompt appears and E opens the PC panel. Close it with CLOSE or Escape, or walk out of reach to close it automatically. The panel consumes repeated interaction and leaves the date/speed controls accessible; text input focus prevents E from opening a station behind the UI. The panel shows website offers, their resource requirements and monthly revenue, and accepted/hosted status. Click ACCEPT, then ASSIGN to put a website on the starter PC. Used/total CPU, RAM, storage, and bandwidth update immediately; the last completed month shows revenue, expenses, and net profit. Disabled controls and inline rejection messages explain unavailable actions. The camera fits the garage below the top HUD and above the status bar as the window resizes. World visuals are labeled prototype art, not a finished art pass.

The hardware station uses the same reach-gated E interaction and Close/Escape behavior as the PC, with its own prefab, blocker, trigger, prompt, and panel shell. The nearest reachable station receives interaction; open panels and UI input prevent opening another station behind them. Loading closes both station panels without moving the player or world. The panel lists all four upgrades with prices, current cash/capacity, and a preview of the changed capacity. BUY calls the shared purchase operation and refreshes both cash displays immediately; OWNED and NEED CASH disable unavailable purchases, while queued invalid actions still show the session rejection reason. Reopening and loading refresh rows against the current session without adding listeners. The PC panel displays current effective capacity and contract demand, including PhotoSnap growth.

Starter content is authored in `Assets/Data/StarterCatalog.asset`, referencing StarterPc, PhotoSnap, and LocalWebsite assets. Create definitions through Assets > Create > Infrastructure Tycoon. Keep explicit content IDs stable when renaming or moving assets. `StarterCatalog.ToRuntime()` validates references and IDs and creates immutable, engine-independent data; resource units are cores, GB, GB, and Mbps. Authored prices use whole dollars and convert to decimal runtime money. GameRoot references this catalog and creates an independent CompanySession with $150 cash, zero operating profit, one starter PC, and no accepted contracts. The session owns the existing clock; hardware runtime IDs are separate from content IDs. ResourceUsage exposes capacity, used demand, and available headroom in the same four units; available headroom clamps to zero for an overused dimension while retaining actual demand. CompanySession.TryAcceptOffer takes a stable offer content ID and returns a new runtime contract; TryAssignContract takes that contract ID and a hardware runtime ID. Both return a success flag and a UI-readable rejection reason. Accepted contracts start unassigned, while hosted contracts consume capacity. AvailableOffers excludes accepted offers. Acceptance and assignment do not change finances. At each outgoing month end, CompanySession bills full authored monthly revenue for hosted contracts and the PC expense once, updating cash and cumulative operating profit. Unassigned contracts earn nothing; there is no proration. BillingHistory and LastMonthlyResult expose the outgoing period, revenue, expenses, and net profit, while each hosted contract tracks PaidMonths. TryBillMonth rejects replayed or invalid periods. HostingPanel and the reusable HostingOfferRow prefab bind these session operations and read-only results to the PC panel.

Phase 3 content is authored in CpuUpgrade, RamUpgrade, StorageUpgrade, InternetUpgrade, and PhotoSnapGrowth assets under Assets/Data, referenced by StarterCatalog. Runtime catalog Upgrades and Milestones are immutable snapshots with globally unique content IDs and validated hardware/offer targets. Upgrade prices/deltas are provisionally $120/+2 cores, $80/+4 GB RAM, $100/+250 GB storage, and $90/+10 Mbps. PhotoSnap growth targets two paid months and a 2-core workload; its other requirements and $100 revenue stay unchanged. Definitions remain separate from session state; the session now applies eligible growth automatically after billing.

HardwareInstance exposes BaseCapacity, EffectiveCapacity, PurchaseRecords, and AppliedUpgradeIds; each immutable UpgradePurchase records hardware runtime ID, upgrade content ID, and the historical amount paid. ContractInstance exposes BaseRequirements, EffectiveRequirements, and AppliedMilestoneIds. Explicit state constructors copy and validate their inputs without charging money or changing another session. ProgressionResources calculates capacity from additive upgrade deltas, selects the latest explicitly applied workload profile by paid-month threshold, and calculates assigned demand/headroom. It retains overload demand above capacity while available headroom clamps to zero. Calculated usage is a returned snapshot; session code owns committing resource state. New companies start with no purchases or applied milestones. Persistence retains applied IDs and silently reconciles eligible missing milestones in older saves without replaying payments or notifications.

CompanySession.TryPurchaseUpgrade(upgradeContentId, hardwareRuntimeId, out reason) now buys an upgrade once for an owned compatible device. It checks affordability and prepares the full capacity/record state before committing; failures return a readable reason without changing cash, profit, assignments, or resource totals. The existing hardware object and workload usage are retained. A fresh CPU purchase costs $120, leaves $30 cash, and raises capacity to 4 cores; an $80 RAM purchase then fails. Purchase costs do not reduce operating profit or change the $20 PC recurring expense. Version 2 saves preserve these transactions, including the amount actually paid. HardwarePanel and its reusable HardwareUpgradeRow prefab expose this operation at the hardware station.

PhotoSnap grows once after its second hosted monthly payment. Billing commits cash, operating profit, paid-month counts, and the monthly ledger before applying the milestone and recomputing assigned demand; MilestoneApplied observers see the fully committed result. CPU demand becomes 2 cores with RAM/storage/bandwidth and $100 monthly revenue unchanged. Both starter sites then use 3/2 cores, or 3/4 with the CPU upgrade; neither assignment is dropped. Unassigned time does not count. Save/load preserves the applied flag and does not replay growth or billing; missing eligible flags from the earlier state-only version 2 build reconcile silently. Contract rows display the effective grown demand and explain the CPU change after two paid months. The PC panel identifies constrained hardware/resources, demand versus capacity, shortfall, and affected contracts. The HUD summarizes constrained resources; a CPU upgrade clears the overload, while unrelated upgrades leave it active. Feedback is derived from current state and reconstructs on reload without replaying growth notifications.

`CompanySession.GetBottlenecks()` derives immutable diagnostic snapshots from current effective demand and capacity. Each report identifies hardware, the constrained resource, demand, capacity, shortfall, units, and assigned contracts. Reports use CPU, RAM, storage, bandwidth order within each device; exact capacity produces no alert. Queries do not change finances or assignments, and reports are reconstructed after loading rather than saved. A CPU upgrade clears the starter CPU overload immediately; a RAM upgrade leaves it active. CapacityFeedback formats these diagnostics for the HUD and PC panel, including CPU/RAM/storage/bandwidth names and readable units. This feedback adds no service penalties or satisfaction state.

Session save serialization is available through SessionSaveJson.Capture(session, gameVersion), ToJson(snapshot), and TryLoad(json, catalog, out restoredSession, out reason). Capture between session updates; capture during a clock boundary callback is rejected. Version 2 preserves the required metadata, decimal finances, fractional-day progress, selected speed, runtime/content IDs, assignments, paid-month counts, and complete billing ledger. Each hardware entry also requires a purchases array (records carry hardware runtime ID, upgrade content ID, and historical amount paid); each contract requires applied_milestone_ids. Applied upgrade IDs are derived from the purchase records. Empty arrays are required when no progression is applied. Capacities and demand are rebuilt from the supplied catalog. Loading validates a separate session before the UI replaces live state. The format still describes one starter PC; recorded upgrade spending reduces cash without reducing operating profit. Valid historical growth overloads are restored, while new assignments retain capacity checks. Version 1 files migrate in memory with no purchases and eligible PhotoSnap growth based on saved paid-month counts. Unsupported future versions and inconsistent progression are rejected. Loading does not rewrite the file; the next explicit SAVE writes version 2 to the same historical filename. Use SAVE and LOAD in the HUD from anywhere in the garage. The single slot is `company-v1.json` under `Application.persistentDataPath`. Saves run after the frame simulation update, flush a temporary sibling file, and atomically replace the previous slot; failures preserve the previous file. Loading closes either station panel and refreshes the clock/cash HUD; reopening the panel rebuilds its controls for the restored company. Player position, station triggers, and panel-open state are not saved. Missing, invalid, unsupported, or inaccessible saves show a short HUD message and leave the current company active. Tests inject unique temporary slots through GameRoot.ConfigureSaveSlot and clean up only those files.

## Phase 2 verified playthrough

1. Open Main in Play Mode for a fresh $150 company. Walk to the PC using WASD/arrows; E stays inactive out of reach and opens the panel within reach.
2. ACCEPT PhotoSnap, then ASSIGN it. Usage becomes 1/2 CPU cores, 2/4 GB RAM, 20/250 GB storage, and 4/10 Mbps. Acceptance alone consumes no resources.
3. Pause on January 31, SAVE, then LOAD. The company, assignment, selected speed, and partial day resume; loading closes the panel. Reopen it with E if still within reach.
4. Resume across February 1. January's result is $100 revenue, $20 expense, and $80 net, leaving $230 cash. SAVE after that payment and LOAD repeatedly: the payment must not repeat.
5. Resume across March 1. February pays once, leaving $310 cash and two paid months for PhotoSnap. Pause and speed controls continue to affect the restored company.

Verified on November 13, 2026 with Unity 6000.6.9f1:

| Suite | Passed |
| --- | --- |
| Edit Mode | 86/86 |
| Editor Play Mode | 20/20 |
| Standalone Windows Play Mode | 20/20 |

`Phase2LoopTests.FreshGameWalkHostSaveBeforeAndAfterBillingThenResumeNextMonth` covers this complete sequence. It drives the keyboard's physics movement motor without teleporting, routes interaction through the E-key handler, and uses UI pointer events (including real raycast checks in the standalone player). Calendar advancement is accelerated deterministically through the clock API; the pre-rollover snapshot preserves exactly 0.75 day progress. Other suites cover every speed's real frame advancement, collisions, reach/re-entry, rejection paths, malformed saves, and failed file replacement.

Run all Edit Mode and Play Mode suites in Window > General > Test Runner. For the standalone check, run the installed Unity editor with `-batchmode -projectPath <repo-root> -runTests -testPlatform StandaloneWindows64 -testResults <results.xml> -logFile <editor.log>`. The integrated test saves `Phase2-Resumed-800x600.png` and `Phase2-Resumed-1280x720.png` under `Application.temporaryCachePath`; both layouts were visually inspected. Tests use unique temporary save directories and clean their slots without touching the player's save.

The garage, player, and PC remain labeled prototype art. Capacity upgrades, PhotoSnap growth, bottleneck alerts, and later progression are outside this verified Phase 2 milestone.

## Phase 3 verified playthrough

1. Start a fresh company, pause, and walk to the PC. ACCEPT and ASSIGN both websites through the panel. Combined demand is 2/2 CPU cores, 3/4 GB RAM, 30/250 GB storage, and 8/10 Mbps. Both stations reject E outside reach.
2. Before growth, pause on February 28 with a partial day and SAVE/LOAD. January has paid once: cash is $310 and operating profit $160. Loading closes panels while preserving the company and player position.
3. Cross March 1 for the second payment. Cash becomes $470 and operating profit $320. PhotoSnap grows from 1 to 2 CPU cores; the PC shows 3/2 cores, affected contracts, and a CPU alert. SAVE/LOAD preserves growth, partial days, and the overload.
4. Walk across the clear floor to the hardware station and BUY the $120 CPU upgrade. Cash becomes $350, operating profit stays $320, capacity becomes 4 cores, and the alert clears. Other upgrades leave a CPU overload active.
5. SAVE/LOAD after purchase, reopen either panel, and continue through April 1. The next month pays exactly $180 revenue less $20 expense: cash becomes $510 and operating profit $480. Usage stays 3/4 cores; purchases and growth do not repeat.
6. In a separate fresh company, buy CPU immediately. The remaining $30 cannot buy the $80 RAM upgrade; even a queued rejected action preserves all saved company state. Loading a legacy version 1 slot also reconstructs eligible growth and permits purchases through the restored panels; the next explicit save writes version 2.

Verified on November 13, 2026 with Unity 6000.6.9f1:

`Phase3LoopTests` covers this integrated sequence, the fresh-company rejection, and legacy migration with restored-panel actions. Movement drives the same physics motor as keyboard input without teleporting; E interaction and UI pointer events perform station actions. Calendar advancement uses the clock API to reach exact checkpoints. Saves before growth, while overloaded, and after purchase compare complete normalized snapshots, including partial days, runtime/content IDs, financial history, purchases, and milestones. The full suites retain Phase 2 regressions and check pointer hit targets, text bounds, and diagnostic separation at 800x600 and 1280x720. `Phase3-Resumed-800x600.png` and `Phase3-Resumed-1280x720.png` under `Application.temporaryCachePath` were visually inspected alongside the existing capacity and upgrade layouts.

Phase 3 is verified with provisional balance values. The garage, player, and station art remain prototypes. Overloads provide diagnostic feedback without service penalties. Tower unlocks, tower purchases, additional hardware, and customer satisfaction remain Phase 4 work; the longer-term tower saving choice has not been playtested.
